# Food-safety temperatures follow the USDA safe minimum internal temperatures
# (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/safe-temperature-chart).

[pasta-water] pasta, spaghetti, noodles, penne, boil, water, salt
Cook pasta in a big pot of water at a rolling boil, salted with about one tablespoon of kosher salt per four quarts of water. Stir in the first minute so it doesn't stick, and don't add oil.

[pasta-finish] pasta, sauce, finish, pasta water, starch, al dente
Save a cup of the starchy pasta water before draining. Cook the pasta a minute short of the package time, then finish it in the sauce for a minute, adding splashes of pasta water so the sauce clings.

[tomato-sauce] tomato, sauce, marinara, garlic, basil, canned tomatoes, pasta
For a quick tomato sauce, warm sliced garlic in olive oil over medium-low heat until fragrant, about a minute, without browning it. Add a can of crushed whole tomatoes, simmer 15 to 20 minutes, season with salt, and add torn basil at the end.

[garlic] garlic, burn, bitter, saute
Garlic burns fast and turns bitter, so cook it over medium-low heat and add liquid or other ingredients as soon as it smells fragrant.

[onions] onion, saute, caramelize, sweat
Onions turn soft and translucent after about five to seven minutes over medium heat. Truly caramelized onions take thirty to forty-five minutes over low heat, stirring now and then.

[rice] rice, white rice, fluffy, steam, ratio
For fluffy white rice, rinse it until the water runs clear, then simmer covered with about one and a half cups of water per cup of long-grain rice for fifteen minutes. Rest it covered, off the heat, for ten minutes before fluffing.

[eggs-boiled] egg, eggs, hard boiled, soft boiled, boil
For hard-boiled eggs, cover them with cold water, bring to a boil, then turn off the heat, cover, and wait ten to twelve minutes before moving them to ice water. For jammy yolks, lower them into boiling water for six to seven minutes.

[eggs-scrambled] egg, eggs, scrambled, omelet, breakfast
For soft scrambled eggs, cook them in butter over low heat, stirring gently, and take them off the heat while they still look a little wet; they keep cooking on the plate.

[searing] sear, brown, steak, crust, pan, meat
For a good brown crust, pat the meat dry, salt it, and get the pan hot before adding oil. Don't crowd the pan, and don't move the meat until it releases on its own.

[chicken] chicken, poultry, breast, thigh, roast chicken, temperature, safety
Cook chicken to 165 degrees Fahrenheit, or 74 Celsius, measured with a thermometer in the thickest part. Let it rest five to ten minutes before cutting so it stays juicy.

[meat-temps] beef, pork, steak, pork chop, roast, lamb, fish, ground beef, temperature, safety
Ground beef and pork are safe at 160 degrees Fahrenheit, or 71 Celsius. Whole cuts of beef, pork, and lamb are safe at 145 Fahrenheit, or 63 Celsius, with a three-minute rest, and fish at 145 Fahrenheit.

[leftovers] leftovers, fridge, refrigerate, store, reheat, safety
Refrigerate leftovers within two hours of cooking, in a fridge at or below 40 degrees Fahrenheit, or 4 Celsius. Reheat them to 165 Fahrenheit.

[cross-contamination] raw meat, cutting board, hands, wash, safety, cross contamination
Use a separate cutting board for raw meat, and wash your hands with soap for twenty seconds after touching it.

[knife] knife, cut, chop, dice, slice, claw, cutting board, safety
Keep your knife sharp; a dull knife slips. Curl your fingertips under in a claw grip, and put a damp towel under the cutting board so it doesn't slide.

[grease-fire] fire, grease fire, oil, smoke, safety
Never put water on a grease fire. Turn off the heat and cover the pan with a lid; a little baking soda also smothers a small fire.

[roast-vegetables] roast, vegetables, broccoli, potatoes, carrots, oven, sheet pan
Roast vegetables at 425 degrees Fahrenheit, or 220 Celsius, tossed in oil and salt, in a single layer with space between pieces, for twenty to thirty-five minutes depending on size. Crowded vegetables steam instead of browning.

[soup-stock] soup, stock, broth, simmer
Keep soups and stocks at a gentle simmer, not a rolling boil, so they stay clear and the meat stays tender.

[seasoning] season, salt, taste, acid, lemon, vinegar, flavor, bland
Season in layers as you cook and taste often. If a dish tastes flat but not under-salted, a squeeze of lemon or a splash of vinegar usually brightens it.

[herbs] herbs, fresh herbs, dried herbs, basil, parsley, oregano, substitute
Dried herbs are stronger than fresh: use about one teaspoon dried for each tablespoon of fresh. Add dried herbs early and delicate fresh herbs at the end.

[buttermilk] buttermilk, substitute, pancakes, milk, lemon, vinegar, baking
To replace a cup of buttermilk, stir one tablespoon of lemon juice or white vinegar into a cup of milk and let it sit five to ten minutes.

[egg-substitute] egg substitute, vegan, flax, baking, substitute
In baking, one tablespoon of ground flaxseed mixed with three tablespoons of water and rested five minutes can replace one egg.

[baking-powder] baking powder, baking soda, leavening, substitute, baking, cake
Baking soda needs an acid, such as buttermilk or yogurt, to rise. To replace one teaspoon of baking powder, use a quarter teaspoon of baking soda plus half a teaspoon of cream of tartar.

[flour] flour, measure, baking, bread, cake, cookies, weigh
For consistent baking, weigh flour: a cup of all-purpose flour is about 120 to 125 grams. If you use cups, spoon the flour into the cup and level it instead of scooping.

[pancakes] pancakes, batter, breakfast, griddle
Mix pancake batter just until combined; a few lumps are fine and overmixing makes them tough. Flip when bubbles pop on the surface and the edges look set.

[yeast] yeast, bread, dough, proof, rise
Activate dry yeast in water around 105 to 110 degrees Fahrenheit; hotter water can kill it. Let dough rise somewhere warm until it roughly doubles.
